My poetry

A reflection on freedom, identity and life

The Architecture of Unbecoming

We are all architects of a beautiful prison,
Building walls out of expectations we never owned,
Chasing the sunrise before the sun has even risen,
Reaping harvest from the fields we never sowed.

We wear the armor of numbers, of status, and name,
Forgetting we are stardust trapped in a fragile frame.

Look at the streets, a synchronized march of the blind,
Running toward a finish line that constantly recedes,
Leaving the warmth of the present moment far behind,
Feeding the ego, while the spirit quietly bleeds.

They buy the script, they memorize the curated lines,
Trapped inside the heavy ink of someone else’s designs.

But I have decided to un-learn the language of the crowd,
To un-belong to the boxes they so neatly drew for me.
I will speak my silence when the world gets too loud,
And anchor my soul in the wild, untamed sea.

Why measure a lifetime by the ticking of a mechanical clock?
When you can measure it by the waves crashing against the rock?

I am not the resume, the degree, or the calculated grin,
I am the unanswered question lingering in the dark.
I am the quiet space where the storms outside begin,
The invisible heat that sleeps inside a dying spark.

Let them keep their empires built on shifting sand,
I would rather rule the chaos of my own untamed land.

For we are ancient beings carrying galaxies in our chest,
Bound by gravity, but destined for the infinite sky.
Why settle for a lifespan that is merely a polite test?
When you have the terrifying privilege to live, and not just try.

So rip off the blueprints, let the old structures fall apart,
The universe doesn't need your perfection—it just wants your heart.
